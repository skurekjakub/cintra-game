# The Lion's Shadow: the witcher spine

Status: v1 (A13, 2026-11-21, decision D8). Binding for the content rewrite wave. It sits beside `story.md` (the user's outline, unchanged: every plot truth there still holds), `gdd.md` §5 (Hale and his kit) and §6.19 (the stigma system), and `content-format.md` §K (grammar).

> **D8, verbatim:** "the main character shud be a witcher obviously, and the entire story should be him getting shit on for being a witcher, duh"

## 1. The spine in one paragraph

Every monster in this story is an animal driven from its home or a weapon left behind by desperate men. **So is he.** Hale is a witcher, made by a fraction of the same dwarven craft that Corvan buried under Karak Ostrel, and the realm treats him the way it treats everything the war left lying around: it fears him, uses him, and blames him. The Pale Choir needs a culprit Cintra will believe, so it pins its crimes on him one by one: the courier, the Queen's poison, the Fair's fire, the stolen Prince. Lord Marshal Kosk hires him for the same reason, because nobody at court has ever bothered to bribe a witcher. The whole game asks one question in a hundred small ways: **will you save a kingdom that spits on you?** It ends at the mountain he was made from, where a boy he dragged out of a knife's reach decides whether Cintra lets him in through the front gate or pays him at the back one.

## 2. Hale (expands gdd §5.1)

- **Hollow Matron.** A dwarf-cut watch-hold on the south shoulder of the Old Grey Matron, above the Weeping Gate. After the Pact, Ostrel's last alchemists took a gentler fraction of the craft that made Corvan's blood-sworn and used it on foundlings and famine children: hunters for the beasts the war had loosed and the blight-casks' leftovers. The hold's sign is the one on his medallion, **a closed door with an open eye above it**: *we watch the door*. It is the same door as on Dravin's seal, the hammer over a closed door. The hold emptied in the 1250s, when the Crown stopped paying the old bounties and the lowland lords stopped hiring "mountain men". Perhaps four Matron witchers still walk.
- **The Trial (1251).** Fourteen boys went into the vats and Hale came out whole. Grey lily was part of the training: the masters fed it to the boys in rising doses until it stopped killing them. He knows its smell, its taste and how long it takes, and that knowledge is what will hang him in every frightened room.
- **Corrick.** An older Matron witcher who went down to the coast in 1251 and never came back. Hale only learns why in the Drowned Rat.
- **Marnadal (1263).** After the battle, Kosk's quartermasters hired him to clear the carrion-beasts from the ditch where the clan dead were left. A dying clansman taught him the naming rite, and against orders he spoke it over the ditch. The clans saw only a cat-eyed thing walking among their dead on Cintran coin, and they call him **the grave-eater**. Sergeant Brask held the ditch line that week. Chaplain Anselm watched from the field chapel. To Anselm, the Marshal paying a mutant to hide a massacre was the whole rot of Cintra's nobility in one picture. He has hated them both, as one sin, ever since.
- **Autumn 1268.** The Eel Row eel-men's guild hired him to clear drowners from the pilots' channel. He cleared it. Gorm Tatch, the guild-master, kept the purse: "We never signed with a witcher." Hale has three ales on Bertha's slate and nowhere to be.
- **His arc.** He starts by keeping the Matron's rule: take the coin, kill the beast, stay out of crowns' business. He ends by bleeding for a crown that has put a price on his head. He does not get loved for it. He gets, at best, a door that opens.

## 3. How the world sees witchers

- **Slurs** (use them in barks, never in narration): *cat-eye*, *vault-get*, *the mountain's bastard*, *mutt*, *grave-eater* (Strept, about Hale himself), *the Marshal's creature* or *the Marshal's monster* (court and Abbey, from Act I on).
- **Superstitions:** a witcher's look sours milk and quickens a cough. They drink babies' breath. A cup a witcher drank from must hang on its own nail. Don't let it touch the dead, or the dead won't rest. Children are told "the cat-eye will take you" at bedtime.
- **Practice:** double prices, a chipped cup, the back door, "kitchen's closed", the gutter instead of a bed. Paid half for a contract, or not at all. Guards stop him at every gate. A town will hang a witcher for a dead cow faster than it will hang a man for a dead wife.
- **What they forget:** the Matron's wardens were once part of the realm's law (§4).

## 4. The secret history: the Warden's Clause

This is new backstory, not a change to any plot truth. The Oath's four verses stay verbatim (`story.md` §8).

When Corvan and the last High King of Ostrel sealed the Deep-Vaults, the Pact had **articles** besides the four spoken verses. One of them, the **Warden's Clause**, reads in the dwarven original: *"The wardens of the Matron who watch the door shall pass every gate of the Lion, eat at every table, and be paid for every beast."* For a century it was obeyed. Then the hold's men became too few to matter, a king's council found the clause expensive, and the clerks left it out of every new copy of the *Book of the Lion*. Nobody remembers it now.

- **Where it surfaces:** Maelis finds a reference in an old charter's index (a1.04 and side.abbey_ledgers). Corvan's shade calls him "my warden" (side.barrows). Dravin knows the craft is Ostrel's (a3.03). The full text is on an iron leaf in Karak Ostrel's Drowned Library (a3.06: `clue.warden_clause`, `flag.warden_clause_found`).
- **The payoff:** at the end, a crowned Aldric can restore it (`flag.warden_clause_restored`). The law changes. Eel Row does not, at least not at once (§9).

## 5. Why he gets the letter

The courier, Tomas Wyle, comes through the Drowned Rat's door foaming grey-green. Everybody in the taproom backs away, because froth means plague to dock folk. **Only the witcher kneels.** Poison does not frighten a body the Matron fed on grey lily, and he does not care what the room thinks of him touching a dying man. Tomas sees the eyes and, half out of his head, takes them for an answer: *"Cat's eyes. You're one of the mountain's. Good. Something's waking. That's your trade."* He gives the letter to the one man in the room who is not afraid of him, and dies in his arms.

So the room sees exactly what it expects: a witcher with his hands on a dead man's throat. From that minute the Choir does not have to invent a culprit. It only has to repeat what Eel Row already believes.

## 7. How the Choir uses the hatred

Anselm's plan in `story.md` needs the court divided and looking the wrong way. A witcher walking into the middle of it is a gift. The frames, in order (gdd §6.19.7 has the binding numbers):

1. **The courier (a1.01).** It isn't planned, only improvised. The room blames him on its own, Cobb says it out loud, and by dawn the man in grey has the ballad-sellers singing *"Witcher poisons the Queen's rider"* in the streets (a1.02).
2. **The vial (a1.03).** At the Lion Steps sword-check, a Choir servant slips a vial into his satchel. It is one of his own elixirs, re-stoppered with Abbey wax. In the sickroom it is "found", given to a dog, and the dog dies, because an elixir kills anything that isn't a witcher. The frame is true enough to be dangerous.
3. **The almonry sermon (a1.04).** Anselm preaches "the Marshal's creature" to the poor he feeds, which turns the Abbey district against Kosk's investigation.
4. **The keeper (a1.06).** The Attre smugglers tell the barricaded widow that a cat-eyed man cut her husband's throat.
5. **The witch-fire (a1.07).** The grease fire is cried as witch-fire. If he uses Ember in the smoke, he proves it for them.
6. **The beast (a2.01).** Tigg's mob trades the halfwit on the gibbet for a better target.
7. **The Prince (a2.08).** A silver-dusted knife and a cat's eye scratched into the bedpost of the empty royal bed, and the oiled catacomb gate left open. Varn issues a warrant within the hour. In Act III his face is on posters at every lowland crossroads.
8. **The songs.** Through all of it, the agitators (gdd §6.19.6) keep the crowds hot. Anselm's journal at Vel Aram (a2.07) holds the page that plans it: *"Let the Marshal's creature carry it. They will believe it of him before they believe it of me."* Read aloud in council (a3.01), that page clears the worst of it.

## 8. The acts, retold

**Act I: Ashes in the Lion's Den.** He arrives as a stranger and leaves as a suspect. Each lead Kosk gives him comes with a crowd that thinks he is the crime. He learns that proof clears him and nothing else does. He also learns that the boy prince, who repeats Anselm's line about witchers' souls, is the one person at court who asks to see his eyes. The act ends with the Fair: he saves Aldric in front of a thousand people who are screaming witch-fire. Kosk sends him south "partly to get you out of my city before they burn you".

**Act II: The Marnadal Road.** Out of the capital, the hatred gets older and simpler. Villages still hang witchers. Bandits and hedge-witches treat him better than lords do. Contracts come back into his life: a wyvern, drowners, a woodwose, a selkie's fog. Each one is a monster that turns out to be a displaced animal or a leftover weapon, like him. At Marnadal Field the clan dead remember the man who said the rite over their ditch. In Vel Aram he reads the page where Anselm planned to use him. Then the Prince is taken, the knife is silver-dusted, and he comes home to a warrant.

**Act III: Under Amell.** Hunted. The lowlands are closed to him, so the mountains are where the help is: the clans who called him grave-eater, the dwarves who made his kind, a wyvern mother he could have killed. In Karak Ostrel he walks through the vats where the blood-sworn were made, and finds the clause that once let men like him pass every gate. In the Lock Chamber, Anselm offers Aldric a choice: *"Look at what comes for you, boy: the thing your ancestors made."* The boy has to choose the witcher.

## 9. The ending: what it means for him

The emotional core is that **he saves a kingdom that spits on him, and the kingdom only partly changes.** The ending resolver (gdd §6.15) decides the realm's fate. The witcher's own fate is told in his closing slides, chosen by `stigma()`, `flag.warden_clause_restored` and `flag.corrick_laid_to_rest`.

| Ending | What it means for the witcher |
|---|---|
| **The Lion Holds** | The court gives Kosk the credit and gives titles to the men who stood behind him. The witcher's reward depends on stigma: paid in the Lion Hall in front of the council (`tolerated`/`shunned`), or paid at the kitchen gate by a clerk who won't touch his hand (`loathed`/`hunted`). If Aldric found and restores the Warden's Clause, the law says he may pass every gate. The last slide is always Bertha's: his cup still hangs on its own nail, and if she turned, she stopped charging double |
| **The Boy King** | The regency council's first act, pressed by Varn, is to bar witchers from the city. A twelve-year-old king can strike it down only if he trusts the witcher and the clause was found. Otherwise Hale leaves by the river gate with his pay and a warrant waiting in every lowland town |
| **The Shattered Lock** | The vault's weapons scatter across the borderlands, and suddenly every village needs a witcher. They pay him now. They still won't let him drink inside |
| **Ashes & Iron** | He holds the passes with the Strept and whatever Sodden sends. The only people who stop spitting on him are the ones who stood in the line beside him |
| **The Bitter Bargain** | The Crown commands the vault, and the vault holds the vats. Kosk's men ask Hale to show Ostrel's alchemists how he was made. Cintra gets a thousand witchers to be its army, and fears every one of them. The kingdom becomes what it hated |

**Corrick's medallion.** If Hale carries Bertha's dead witcher's medallion to Hollow Matron's cairn above the Weeping Gate (a3.06), `flag.corrick_laid_to_rest` adds a slide: one more Matron's man brought home. It is the only slide that is about witchers and not about Cintra.

## 12. Calls made in this spine (for review)

1. **"Hale" kept** as the default name (gdd §5.1 gives the reason). The player can still rename him. The identity is fixed.
2. **The Matron's School is original.** It is tied to this world's own lore (Ostrel's craft, the Old Grey Matron, the closed-door seal), and it uses no animal-school naming.
3. **The witcher craft descends from the blood-sworn.** This is additive backstory, and no `story.md` truth changes. It makes the vault personal, and it gives the Bitter Bargain its sharpest meaning.
4. **The Warden's Clause** is a new article of the Pact, not a verse. The Oath stays verbatim.
5. **Stigma never closes a required milestone.** Every frame has a proof-based clear. Infamy has a floor of 10, so the realm never fully forgives.
